When AI Becomes a Research Assistant: Building transparent, controllable and trustworthy Agent UX in a national-level IM App

AI; Motion Design Dec 2025 ~12 min read

Context & Vision

Recent AI systems such as GPT, Claude and Gemini have introduced Deep Research agents — systems capable of autonomously planning, searching, reasoning and synthesizing information to generate expert-level reports.

Meanwhile, WeChat stands as a national-scale social platform with 1.3 billion daily active users, serving a population that spans all demographics and walks of life. Our mission in this project was to bridge these two worlds: integrating the power of Deep Research into the WeChat ecosystem.

While DeepResearch was initially conceived for professional analysis, we aim to introduce this power into the WeChat ecosystem to achieve "equal access to information" for all users, such as

  • helping seniors compare medication side effects
  • supporting parents in evaluating career prospects
  • assisting small business owners with startup cost analysis

Challenge

My core challenges stemmed from two fundamental areas of misalignment:

1.The Technical Misalignment: Deep Research vs. Conventional AI

Process Latency & Opaque States:While Conventional AI models generate responses within a minute, Deep Research processes can take 10 to 15 minutes. Without proper transparency, this prolonged “black-box” latency creates significant user anxiety.

Output Cognitive Overload：Conventional AI delivers easily digestible text snippets. In contrast, Deep Research generates comprehensive, professional-grade reports, creating a steep cognitive barrier for everyday users.

2.The Ecological Misalignment: WeChat vs. Standalone AI apps

Differences in user mindset: Standalone AI apps cater to tech-savvy, early adopters who are tolerant of steep learning curves. WeChat, however, serves a mass-market, all-age demographic where the tolerance for complex UI is extremely low, and interactions must be purely intuitive.

Contextual Fragmentation: Standalone AI products are usually entered with a clear goal and left once the task is done; in WeChat, however, user behavior is “interruption-driven”. Users tend to ask questions in the gaps between social and everyday moments, and may drop out at any time.

The core challenge lies in transforming a long, complex, and opaque backend process into a transparent, controllable, and trustworthy experience for WeChat users.

Technical Understanding

Before designing the UX, I analyzed the underlying workflow of Deep Research. Unlike the linear 'question-inference process - Answer' paradigm of conventional AI, Deep Research functions as an autonomous, multi-stage agent.The process begins with intent parsing and problem decomposition, leading to an initial research framework. Following this, the agent initiates an iterative execution cycle: it performs autonomous inference - generate task - invokes tools(search/read/analyze)-task summary - inference based on the newly obtained information to generate the next task - ... This looping mechanism continues iteratively until the agent has fulfilled all requirements and finalized the comprehensive research report.

Throughout this iterative research process, the system generates and aggregates a massive volume of data. To determine the optimal presentation strategy, I evaluated each element based on both user value and experiential efficiency:

Core Elements (Enhancing Perception & Sense of Control)

  • Research Outline:：Visible. Serving as a 'table of contents,' it helps users build a mental model of the research scope, compensating for the lack of direct participation in the planning phase.
  • Task Name:Visible. This allows users to track the current research stage with clarity.
  • Search Keywords: ：Visible. Displaying the search trajectory demonstrates the 'depth' and transparency of the research process.
  • Source Citations:：Visible. Presenting information through titles and source origins proves the 'breadth' of the study and allows users to cross-verify findings against primary sources.

Efficiency & Noise Reduction (Balancing Information vs. Cognitive Load)

  • Inference Monologue :：Hidden. This content is often verbose and low-value for mainstream users. Hiding it minimizes visual clutter and focuses the user's attention on key actionable insights.

Fields requiring further verification (balancing speed and periodic feedback)

  • Task Summaries: Displaying summaries can alleviate the anxiety of long waits, but outputting summaries will increase the time taken and delay the progress towards the final report. It is necessary to find the optimal balance between speed and user experience.

Based on these insights, we have architected a DeepResearch interaction framework optimized for WeChat.

Key Design Solutions

Based on our understanding of technology, in response to the above challenges, we have proposed our solutions from four aspects: product framework, process animation, report design, and WeChat scenario adaptation.

Interaction Architecture

In the framework design, I tried multiple versions of the plan. At the early stage of the project, I clearly proposed that the traditional 'linear streaming typing' layout is not suitable for DeepResearch. This is because the tasks, actions, and conclusions of DeepResearch all involve a large amount of information, and the structure of linear typing will cause users to need to digest a huge amount of text information. Moreover, when the screen is filled with long text, users are highly likely to lose their sense of the research process and be unable to determine which stage of the overall task they are currently in.

A simpler solution is to fold the content of each stage, which can ensure that the structure of the research stage is stably visible and users will not lose track of the current research stage. However, another drawback of this approach is that due to the long duration of DeepResearch and the small content display area under this structure, users have to wait for more than ten minutes facing a page that hardly changes, which is undoubtedly a catastrophic experience.

To balance the need for sufficient content display area and the visibility of the research framework, we introduced a pinned tab in the third attempt:

However, this design has given rise to new problems: first, information redundancy caused by repeated steps; second, interaction conflicts between horizontal step switching and vertical content scrolling.

To address this, we transformed the step flow from horizontal to vertical, integrating it with the original steps in the content flow to form a vertically stacked drawer-style interaction. This design simultaneously achieved three goals: information simplification, exposed research framework, and providing sufficient display space for research content.

Why do I emphasize so much the exposure of the research framework steps? Because the usage scenarios of WeChat are more diverse compared to standalone AI app. Such a design can:

  • For first-time users, the design ensures they can immediately grasp the key stages of Deep Research and understand how the agent operates at a glance.
  • For returning users accessing via history, the interface instantly anchors them to the current research progress upon entry.
  • For shared-link recipients, the experience seamlessly introduces them to the research methodology while providing immediate access to the final generated report.

Process Design

As previously mentioned, the research duration of deepresearch is 10 times that of other AI models, approximately 10-15 minutes. We need to find a suitable solution to transform the long wait into an observable process with progress and anticipation.

Animation

The first version of the process animation design fully simulates human behavior, manifested as the AI searching for results based on keywords, then clicking to enter and read, returning to the search results, clicking on other articles to enter and read, and repeating this cycle.

Although this version of the animation is very close to human behavior and users can easily understand what the AI is doing, it is disconnected from the AI's real behavior and has many ineffective repetitive actions.

So, in the second version, I made "logical alignment" and adjusted the animation to continuous searching followed by continuous reading according to the real behavior of AI:

However, such animations inadvertently created the illusion that "AI is stupid." During our internal user testing, we frequently received feedback that "AI feels slow and not very intelligent." Through deeper user analysis and competitive research, we identified that the reason lies in the fact that users' mental model of AI is "superhuman efficiency," while the behavior pattern of simulating human "reading article by article" appears cumbersome and inefficient in perception. To find the balance between technical efficiency and human perception, I tried to apply a fade-in and fade-out animation from bottom to top to the "high-value information nodes" during the AI operation, such as searching for keywords, finding and analyzing an article, while the specific article content is quickly skipped over by typing.

Graphical animations simulating human behavior are used at key information nodes to ensure understandability, while the fast typing effect of machine language is retained for low-value information to meet users' psychological expectations of "AI efficiency." This "combination of speed and slowness" strategy not only ensures process transparency but also reflects the efficiency of the intelligent agent.

We also tried to layer an expression of “speed” on top of the graphical animation, but it was not shipped in the end due to project timeline constraints and design expression guidelines.

Trade-off: whether to output task summaries with a typing animation during the research process

Outputting summaries inevitably consumes additional time—the model needs to synthesize, refine, and organize the information gathered in each round, then present it word by word. Every summary has the potential to delay the delivery of the final report by dozens of seconds or even longer. By contrast, skipping summaries and proceeding directly to the next task or the final report would undoubtedly allow users to access the final reoport more quickly.

In the early stages, we prioritized efficiency and opted not to output summaries. However, during actual user testing, we discovered something important: in the context of a research process lasting over ten minutes, the time consumed by summaries is almost negligible. What truly matters is that these periodic summaries naturally segment the lengthy process—each summary serves as a cognitive resting point, allowing users to cycle through a rhythm of 「anticipation → observation → reward → respite」, transforming anxious waiting into a gratifying progression.

As a result, we reintroduced task summaries into the final design.

Report Design

Considerable effort was put into fine-tuning the citations, data graphics, and typesetting to ensure a premium reading experience. I'll leave those details aside for now.

Moving on to the report’s generation and presentation: since the large volume of content in the report, AI also requires users to wait for a relatively long time during the writing process. Meanwhile, we believe that building initial context is key to maintaining user patience. Based on this judgment, we resolutely abandoned various Progress Bar loading solutions.

The first iteration proposed that while the AI generates the report, the main title framework would appear progressively via typing animation at the report entry point, with a skeleton screen indicating loading below the titles. The full report would then be revealed and made accessible upon completion.

While the initial solution was functional, it lacked intuitiveness and risked creating false expectations—such as users mistakenly trying to scroll the window or expecting to read the full report directly.

In the optimized version, we introduced an AI-generated summary (under 200 words) that renders via typing animation. Compared to section headings, the summary provides a more intuitive understanding of the report's content. With this preview, users are far less likely to feel overwhelmed by the sheer volume of information when they eventually open the full report.

WeChat Scenario Adaptation

As previously mentioned, the user context for triggering Deep Research within WeChat differs fundamentally from that of a standalone AI product. WeChat is primarily a high-frequency, real-time social and lifestyle platform where users engage with AI during fragmented moments. Forcing users to remain on a single screen for a ten-minute-plus wait — effectively disrupting their primary social interactions — is practically unfeasible.

Consequently, we developed a tailored solution for 'interruption and recovery,' ensuring that Deep Research seamlessly integrates into the user's social flow rather than obstructing it.

Interruption and Recovery

At the start of each query, we inform users that “the research will continue even if you leave the page”; Additionally, when users first exit, an educational half-screen guides them to find their ongoing research in the history; and within the history list, each DeepResearch task is clearly labeled with its status—「In Progress」 or 「Report Ready」 (indicating that the research report has been generated).

However, this approach only helps users locate their research within the search scenario. In the broader WeChat context, we introduced a global floating window that appears when users exit DeepResearch. It provides real-time progress updates directly within the window. And a distinctive animation be used to signal when the report is complete. Users can then return to their research anytime, directly from the floating window, without disrupting their chat.

Reflection

Reflecting on the entire design process, our core challenge lay in two critical shifts: the AI’s "thinking time" jumped from seconds to over ten minutes, while the user’s "usage scenarios" pivoted from focused engagement to fragmented interaction.

Our solution was to reshape the AI from a "black-box respondent" into a "visualized collaborator":

  • Transparency: Allow users to see the "research path" of AI, transforming long waiting periods into observable processes;
  • Controllable: Through a drawer-based framework and a global floating window, we enabled freely enter and exit. This ensures users can step away during a chat and return effortlessly to keep the progress under control.
  • Trustworthy: During the report generation phase, by presenting the summary first and gradually unfolding the content, users can form expectations about the content before clicking to read, avoiding being overwhelmed by a deluge of information, and thus truly willing to read and trust the report.

Data feedback also proves the effectiveness of this solution: during the gray-scale testing (Oct 13 – Oct 19), usage QV increased by 28%, the average time on page reached as long as 381 seconds, the report view rate rose from 72.08% to 79.47%, and the report sharing rate increased from 34.55% to 40.96%. This is not only a growth in data but also represents that through our design, every user can equally and efficiently enjoy the decision-making assistance brought by AI in WeChat.
